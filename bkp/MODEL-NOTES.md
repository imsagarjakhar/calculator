# Legacy calculator model notes

These notes describe the original static calculators retained in this folder. Relative file paths and direct Node commands below assume `bkp` is the working directory. For the current React application use the root README and MIGRATION.md.

## Search visibility

The production domain in `CNAME` is `intimetec.org`. Both pages include unique titles and descriptions, canonical HTTPS URLs, sharing metadata and JSON-LD page information. The home page also declares the Money Planner site name and includes visible descriptions of the calculators. `robots.txt` permits crawling and points to `sitemap.xml`, which lists the three indexable pages. Growth and Survival are views of the home page, not separate sitemap URLs.

Deploy `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml` at the domain root along with the production HTML, CSS and JavaScript files. If the production domain changes, update `CNAME`, both pages' canonical/sharing/JSON-LD URLs, and the robots/sitemap URLs together.

After publishing, verify ownership of `https://intimetec.org/` in [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/), submit `https://intimetec.org/sitemap.xml`, and inspect/request indexing for the home, Custom Plan and Financial Freedom URLs. Verification requires the domain or Search Console owner; do not add a fabricated verification token. Check that the live pages return HTTP 200, HTTPS works, and alternate hosts redirect to the canonical domain. Search Console reports indexing and actual search performance. Crawl permission, sitemap submission and structured data do not guarantee indexing or ranking. Useful, accurate content and relevant links to the calculators can support discovery; avoid keyword stuffing or purchased links.

- **Check Growth:** starting assets, loans, SIP, annual step-up, extra investments, life expenses, and a net wealth goal.
- **Check Survival:** current corpus, age, monthly SWP, inflation, effective annual return, and end age, with estimated funded days, depletion age, a chart, yearly balances, and CSV download.
- **Financial Freedom:** salary, working costs, available investments, desired retirement spending and future expenses, with the earliest modelled stop-work age, a preferred-age funding gap and sensitivity comparisons.

Survival spreads each month's SWP budget across its plan-month days, compounds the effective annual return daily, and increases the budget on each plan anniversary. It includes leap days and partial final-day coverage. It is a forward projection with fixed assumptions, not a historical backtest. Detailed assumptions are available below the calculator.

Survival's visible goal is to fund the selected age horizon; spending down the starting corpus is allowed. The results also show the present value needed to cover that exact daily spending/event schedule and the final balance's approximate purchasing power at today's prices. `requiredCorpus` discounts events at day-start and regular spending after each day's growth, using the same schedule as the forward engine. Financial Freedom's ongoing-income goal is stricter: it aims to retain the income pot's purchasing power rather than deliberately exhaust it at the horizon. Its results explicitly show this goal, the active buffer percentage and the number/total of included expenses. These planners use separate expense lists and different daily/monthly inflation timings. Compare the same starting age, spending base date, returns, inflation, buffer and expenses before comparing targets.

Inputs are stored locally when browser storage is available. Growth and Survival keep separate inputs. Currency amounts in Survival are entered directly without conversion.

Both calculators support optional life expenses: child education, weddings, home/car purchases, medical costs, renovations, travel, emergencies, and custom events. Set a plan year/month and enter either today's cost (inflated to the event month) or the future cost. Edit, pause/include, or remove entries to compare scenarios. Growth and Survival save separate expense lists for each currency; the Growth Reset button clears its lists across all currencies. The PPT example and Survival example resets retain life expenses.

Growth pays life expenses after each month's SIP, extra deposits, and EMIs; today's costs are inflated through that month-end. Unfunded costs are reported without creating debt; later SIP contributions continue. Survival pays expenses at the start of their plan month and stops at corpus depletion. Its unfunded summary includes later scheduled costs. The summaries compare each plan with the same assumptions and no life expenses. Yearly/monthly tables and CSV exports include funded expenses and shortfalls.

Growth also supports multiple existing assets (each with its own effective annual return / CAGR and withdrawal availability) and loans (outstanding principal, nominal annual interest, original total tenure, remaining months, automatically calculated EMI, and payment source). Net wealth equals all projected assets minus remaining loans; available funds exclude assets marked not for withdrawal. Income-funded EMIs do not reduce the investment pool: enter the SIP after EMI commitments. Loan EMI is calculated from the outstanding balance and remaining months using a reducing-balance annuity (or balance ÷ remaining months at 0% interest). Total tenure records the original term and must be at least the remaining term. EMI displays two decimal places; projections use full precision. Loan summaries show both the original schedule and the estimated months needed to repay debt at the end of the projection. Missed investment-funded payments can extend repayment. Previously saved manual-EMI loans are preserved; edit and enter tenure to switch them to automatic calculation. This assumes on-time monthly payments with no prepayments, fees, or rate changes. The current-balance mode remains available for lender-confirmed figures. Fully paid loans have zero future debt and EMI. Run `node original-loan.test.js` for independent amortisation comparisons and form checks.


## Custom Plan

The home page now links to `custom-plan.html`, a separate monthly planner with separate browser storage. Growth and Survival calculations keep their existing behavior.

1. Enter your starting and end months using the standard month inputs, then enter assets, loans, default monthly SIP, and SIP destination. The duration is calculated automatically and includes both selected months. New investment accounts can start at zero.
2. In the monthly table, override any month's SIP (including zero to skip). A blank override uses the default SIP and annual step-up.
3. Choose a month and add extra investments to specific assets, loan prepayments, or one-time expenses. For example, leave the SIP at 20,000 and add a 50,000 bonus into a Stocks account.
4. Use **Save JSON** to download the complete editable plan as versioned JSON, or **Download report** for a readable, standalone HTML report. **Import plan / report** accepts either file, validates its embedded plan and previews it before replacing the open Custom Plan. CSV and printed PDF copies cannot restore the plan.
5. **Copy Growth setup** previews a copy of saved Growth inputs, assets, loans, extras and enabled life expenses. It reads the existing data without changing it. Older manual-EMI loans need remaining tenure entered in Growth first. The copied SIP account uses an equivalent effective annual return to preserve Growth's nominal annual-rate / 12 calculation.

Monthly cash flows retain their calendar dates when the plan start or duration changes. Entries outside the projection are kept and excluded. Import does not advance balances or dates automatically. If you update a current balance later, also update the starting month to match that balance date. Starting-plan fields recalculate and save valid edits immediately, including dates, SIP, annual step-up, inflation and SIP destination. Incomplete or invalid baseline edits retain the last valid results and disable downloads until corrected. Asset, loan and cash-flow forms still require Add/Save before exporting.

The **Plan currency** selector in the Custom Plan header supports INR, SAR, USD, AED, EUR and GBP. Selection saves immediately and updates results, chart amounts and future reports. This sets the unit for the entire plan without converting or clearing entered amounts. Unapplied form edits remain available. Imported plans restore their saved currency; Reset restores INR.

Custom Plan supports 1–720 months, up to 50 assets, 50 loans and 2,000 dated cash flows. All asset returns are effective annual assumptions compounded monthly. End-of-month deposits start earning in the following month. Scheduled EMIs precede extra loan repayments and expenses. Prepayments keep EMI unchanged and shorten payoff; requests above outstanding debt are capped and the unused amount is not invested automatically. Income-funded payments assume external cash is available. Investment-funded payments use only withdrawable assets. Unfunded payments remain in loans; unfunded one-time costs do not create debt.

Current loans default to original amount and dates: enter the starting amount, annual interest, total tenure in years plus months, loan start date, and current balance date. Completed monthly anniversaries determine paid months; the first EMI is assumed one month after the start date, with month-end clamping. The form calculates remaining EMI count, monthly EMI, estimated outstanding principal, and total remaining payments including interest. These estimates assume all payments were made on time at a fixed rate. For past prepayments, missed payments, or rate changes, use the current outstanding balance method with lender-confirmed figures. Align the plan's starting month with the balance date. Saved loan dates and original details remain editable after import; existing balance-only loans remain supported. Run `node custom-loan.test.js` for date boundaries, independent amortisation, payoff, and saved-file checks.

For GitHub Pages, include the new production files `custom-plan.html`, `custom-plan.css`, `custom-engine.js`, and `custom-plan.js`, plus the updated `index.html` and `planner.css`, along with the existing calculator files. Custom Plan accepts a **Target amount (today’s value)** in the starting-plan section. Today’s value refers to prices at the plan start, so saved plans retain the same reference date. The end target is `targetToday * (1 + inflation / 100) ** (months / 12)`, using the inclusive projection duration. Both nominal and inflation-adjusted net worth cards show their matching target and shortfall/surplus. The target saves with JSON/HTML reports; older version-1 files without it receive the prior 10-million target, in their selected currency. Enter 0 to omit target comparisons. Changing the target does not change contributions or projected returns.

The offline report uses the same summary definitions. Unfunded cash flows remain a separate warning. Investment performance excludes debt balances and income-funded loan payments; investment-funded expenses and loan payments count as portfolio withdrawals. XIRR dates starting holdings immediately before the starting month and all other cash flows at month-end, using actual days / 365. It is projected performance from plan start, not historical purchase performance. Missing or multiple numerical solutions show no single XIRR. Real net worth discounts ending net worth by annual inflation over the plan months; real gain discounts each investment, withdrawal and terminal asset value to plan-start prices. ## Financial Freedom

The fourth main-page card opens `freedom.html`. The main form asks for current age, current corpus, monthly SIP, and desired monthly spending at today's prices. Only three main assumptions are shown: yearly SIP step-up, inflation, and one expected annual return. The same return applies while building the corpus and after freedom. A slider lets users change the return and see the age and monthly support update immediately. The result compares the inflation-adjusted first monthly bill after freedom with the monthly amount the corpus could support.

Under **02 / The life you want**, the investment schedule toggles between Yearly and Monthly. Yearly shows the monthly SIP for each plan year, contribution-month count, yearly investments and cumulative new investments. Monthly lists every contribution through the estimated freedom month, or through the search horizon if freedom is not reached. It shows partial final contribution years and a zero-contribution state if the starting corpus already meets the target. Totals exclude the starting corpus, growth and expenses. The schedule refreshes with inputs and currency changes, without changing the calculations.

Salary forecasting and separate working/retirement return controls have been removed. Current salary/spending, a preferred stop-work age, the search end age, an optional minimum corpus and buffer are inside a closed extra-settings section. Salary is only a current affordability check and does not cap or forecast future contributions. The entered SIP and yearly step-ups are assumed funded; a warning identifies a SIP above today's available salary budget. Working living expenses are not subtracted from the SIP again. Unallocated salary is outside the model. Users must be able to fund future step-ups.

The income-based target aims to maintain the regular-income pot's purchasing power in a constant-return illustration, rather than deliberately spend the corpus to zero at a finite age. If `q=(1+expectedReturn/100)^(1/12)` and `i=(1+inflation/100)^(1/12)`, the required spending corpus at month `m` is `desiredMonthlyToday*i^(m+1)/(q-i)`. This is the present value of a perpetually inflation-linked monthly withdrawal stream. With positive monthly spending and return at or below inflation, there is no finite target; the UI explains this and avoids invalid chart coordinates. It does not assert that a finite retirement drawdown is impossible.

Future one-off expenses are reserved separately using their discounted costs. At month `m`, `reserve[m]=(reserve[m+1]+eventCost[m+1])/q`, with zero terminal event reserve at the search horizon. The target is the spending pot plus the remaining event reserve, multiplied by the optional buffer, or the optional inflation-adjusted minimum corpus if larger. Only scheduled one-offs within the search horizon are included; the regular-income target continues beyond it. Default buffer/minimum are zero.

Before freedom, the portfolio earns the expected monthly return, receives the stepped-up SIP at month-end, and pays scheduled future expenses. After the first fully funded month, SIPs stop and inflation-linked living withdrawals begin, using the same expected return. Earlier unpaid events disqualify subsequent crossing dates; those commitments are not silently forgiven. The chart and yearly/monthly schedules illustrate the path up to the search end age. Required targets are shown only until freedom. Monthly support is calculated after reserving future events and any optional buffer, allowing the remaining pot to grow with inflation.

The return is a total-return assumption, not a guaranteed monthly cash payout; withdrawals may require selling investments. This model does not capture volatility/sequence risk or calculate pensions, borrowing, taxes, insurance, or fees separately. Use return assumptions after relevant costs. The age is a planning illustration, not a promise or a recommendation to quit work. Background: [Investor.gov: purchasing power](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/purchasing-power).

Future expenses have a separate `freedom` namespace per currency; inputs use `intimetec-freedom-v1`. **Reset inputs** keeps future-expense lists. Old saved plans retain their SIP settings and migrate to a single expected return using the former after-work rate (or the former working rate if no after-work rate is present). A message asks users to review the changed ongoing-income calculation, which can differ substantially from the previous finite drawdown estimate. Very old plans lacking SIP fields derive initial SIP/step-up from their former surplus/growth assumption. Explicit zero SIP values remain zero.
