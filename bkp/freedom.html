How this estimate works

The regular-income target is a growing monthly withdrawal stream under a constant-return illustration. If r is the monthly growth factor from your expected annual return and i is the monthly inflation factor, the spending corpus required at a candidate date is the next month’s desired spending divided by (r − i). This allows the income pot’s purchasing power to keep pace with inflation. With positive spending and return at or below inflation, there is no finite target under this model. This does not mean retirement is impossible; it means this ongoing-income assumption is not supported.

We separately reserve the present value of included future one-off expenses. The optional buffer is added to the combined requirement. If larger, an optional minimum corpus inflated to the candidate month becomes the target. Each month’s investment is your SIP increased by the yearly step-up, added after investment growth. Future expenses are paid at month-end. We do not invest all salary left after expenses or subtract regular working expenses again from the entered SIP.

The first month with enough corpus and no earlier unpaid future expense becomes the estimated freedom date. New SIPs stop the following month. The expected return is unchanged, and the portfolio pays your inflation-adjusted monthly spending and later expenses. The chart and table stop at the search end age; the income target itself assumes continuing spending beyond it. Only scheduled one-off expenses within that horizon are included.

Monthly support is estimated after setting aside the remaining one-off expense reserve, the optional buffer, and the amount needed to maintain the income pot’s purchasing power. Salary is used only for today’s budget check. You need to fund the planned investment step-ups from your future income. Future expenses are paid from the investment pot rather than unspecified spare salary.

This is not a guaranteed yield, a probability of success or a recommendation to leave work. Real returns fluctuate; withdrawing from investments may require selling units. Sequence risk, taxes, pensions, fees and borrowing are not separately modelled. Use an expected return after relevant costs and review the plan as circumstances change.
